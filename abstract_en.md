# Abstract

How public acceptance of personal data use during disasters is associated with governance perceptions remains a central issue in data-intensive governance. This study examines how managerial trust, risk perception, and perceived safe management are associated with policy acceptance in Korean disaster data governance, where command-and-control authority coexists with post-regulatory accountability mechanisms. Because the original q25 item is coded as `1=Yes` and `2=No`, it is recoded as `safe_management=1` when respondents perceive that their personal data are safely managed and `0` otherwise.

Using cross-sectional survey data from 1,094 Korean citizens, the study estimates OLS regression models and uses HC3 robust standard errors and ordered logit models as robustness checks. The results show that perceived safe management is positively associated with policy acceptance (β=0.191***). Managerial trust also shows a relatively large positive unstandardized association with policy acceptance (β=0.713***). Secondary analyses indicate an indirect association pattern through institutional trust and a conditional association in which the managerial trust-policy acceptance slope is steeper under higher perceived risk.

The central contribution is comparative: perceived safe management is positively associated with policy acceptance, while relational and competence-based managerial trust also shows a comparatively large observed association with public acceptance and perceived governance legitimacy.

**Keywords:** governance legitimacy, disaster data governance, hybrid governance, managerial trust, perceived safe management, risk perception, policy acceptance
