Governments increasingly rely on personal data to respond to infectious diseases, natural hazards, and other disasters. Because such practices depend on public cooperation, policy acceptance is central to the sustainability of disaster data governance. Although privacy calculus and trust have both been used to explain citizens' acceptance of personal-data use, they have generally been examined separately rather than within a single analytical framework. This study examines how management trust, positive perception of safe management, and risk perception are associated with policy acceptance of personal-data use for disaster response, and whether the association between management trust and policy acceptance varies by perceived risk. Drawing on data from a cross-sectional national survey of 1,094 Korean citizens, this study applies OLS regression together with mediation and moderation analyses. Management trust, defined as judgments regarding the necessity and effectiveness of data use, was positively associated with policy acceptance. Positive perception of safe management was also positively associated with policy acceptance, whereas risk perception was negatively associated with acceptance in the primary specification. The positive association between management trust and policy acceptance became stronger as risk perception increased, and the conditional indirect association through institutional trust also became stronger at higher levels of perceived risk. The findings suggest that public acceptance of governmental data use may be associated not only with perceived safety but also with citizens' judgments about its necessity and effectiveness.
