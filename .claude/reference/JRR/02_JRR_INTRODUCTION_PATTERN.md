Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Derive the structural writing conventions of JRR introductions for future manuscript preparation.

---

# 1. Objective of the Introduction

The Introduction in JRR is **not** designed primarily to review literature.

Instead, it performs one overarching function:

> Convince the reader that an unresolved societal risk problem exists and that answering one specific scientific question will improve understanding of that problem.

Accordingly, JRR introductions are problem-driven rather than technology-driven or theory-driven.

---

# 2. Canonical Introduction Structure

Across the reviewed papers, the dominant sequence is:

```

Real-world risk problem

↓

Why this problem matters

↓

Current knowledge

↓

Remaining uncertainty

↓

Research gap

↓

Research objective / RQ

↓

Contribution

```

This sequence appears with remarkable consistency.

---

# 3. Paragraph Functions

## Paragraph 1

### Establish the societal problem

Purpose:

Introduce an ongoing or emerging societal risk.

Typical examples observed:

- pandemic
- climate risk
- AI risk
- disaster communication
- misinformation
- uncertainty

Importantly,

JRR rarely begins with:

> "Technology has rapidly developed..."

Instead,

it begins with

> "Society faces..."

or

> "Public decision making increasingly depends on..."

Example pattern observed in uploaded papers:

AI is increasingly embedded into disaster response infrastructures before moving to the communication problem. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

COVID-19 paper begins with the pandemic and public concern rather than psychological theory. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Paragraph 2

### Explain why the problem is difficult

This paragraph introduces uncertainty.

Typical themes:

- uncertainty
- complexity
- conflicting evidence
- imperfect information
- public trust
- institutional response

Purpose:

Convince readers that the problem cannot be solved simply.

---

## Paragraph 3

### Review existing knowledge

Characteristics:

Not comprehensive.

Only literature necessary to position the gap.

Typical writing pattern:

Previous studies have shown...

Existing research suggests...

Recent work indicates...

No historical review.

No exhaustive summary.

---

## Paragraph 4

### Literature Gap

This is arguably the most important paragraph.

Observed pattern:

```

Existing studies

↓

However

↓

Little is known

↓

Specifically

↓

Therefore

```

Gap types found repeatedly:

### Type A

Existing studies focus on one mechanism.

Missing another mechanism.

---

### Type B

Existing studies examine one context.

Missing another context.

---

### Type C

Existing studies explain outcomes.

Do not explain underlying processes.

---

The uploaded AI disaster paper illustrates this clearly:

Research has increasingly examined AI in disaster management, yet little is known about how the public interprets, trusts, and responds to AI-led disaster alerts. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# 4. JRR Gap Construction Rules

JRR almost never uses:

- "No Korean study..."
- "Few domestic studies..."
- "There is no research in Korea..."

Instead,

Gap is almost always framed as

scientific knowledge gap.

Examples:

Current literature has not compared...

Little is known about...

Existing evidence remains mixed...

Previous studies overlook...

---

# 5. Research Question Placement

Observed pattern:

Gap

↓

Immediately

↓

Research objective

↓

Research question

No long transition.

No methodological discussion.

The AI disaster article moves directly from the identified gap to explicit RQs and hypotheses.

---

# 6. Contribution Pattern

Contribution is concise.

Usually presented as

First,

Second,

Third,

or equivalent prose.

Typical contribution dimensions:

## Theoretical

Extend

Integrate

Clarify

Explain

---

## Methodological

New context

New comparison

New experiment

New dataset

---

## Practical

Risk communication

Policy

Public management

Decision making

Emergency response

---

# 7. Theory Usage in the Introduction

Important observation.

Theory is introduced only enough to motivate the study.

Detailed theory is postponed.

Introduction should NOT become a theory section.

Theoretical integration occurs later.

---

# 8. Tone

Highly restrained.

Preferred verbs:

suggest

indicate

examine

investigate

explore

assess

evaluate

Less frequent:

prove

demonstrate

confirm

establish

JRR avoids overly definitive language in introductions.

---

# 9. Language Style

Characteristics

✓ concise

✓ evidence-based

✓ low rhetorical intensity

Avoid:

remarkable

dramatic

revolutionary

extremely important

Instead:

growing

emerging

persistent

underexplored

limited

mixed evidence

# 11. Paper Organization Paragraph

Usually one short paragraph.

Example structure:

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows...

Never longer than necessary.

---

# 12. Editor Notes

From the reviewed papers, the following appear to characterize JRR introductions:

✓ Real societal problem before theory

✓ Scientific gap rather than geographic gap

✓ Explicit uncertainty

✓ Mechanism-oriented framing

✓ Concise literature review

✓ Early statement of contribution

✓ Conservative language

---

# 13. Implications for Our Manuscript

Our manuscript already aligns with several JRR conventions:

Strengths

- problem-driven framing
- mechanism-oriented moderation model
- literature gap based on competing explanations

Recommended improvements

Open with the substantive policy problem before introducing AI or data-driven government as a technology.

State the scientific uncertainty earlier.

Compress the literature review.

Keep detailed theoretical discussion inside the Theory section rather than the Introduction.

Rewrite the contribution as three explicit contributions rather than embedding it in narrative paragraphs.

---

# 14. JRR Introduction Checklist

Before submission, verify that the Introduction satisfies all items below.

□ Opens with a real societal risk problem.

□ Explains why the problem matters.

□ Reviews only directly relevant literature.

□ States a scientific knowledge gap.

□ Avoids "no Korean studies" as the primary gap.

□ Identifies unresolved uncertainty.

□ States clear research objective.

□ Presents contribution explicitly.

□ Uses cautious, non-causal language.

□ Defers detailed theory to the Theory section.

□ Ends with a brief paper organization paragraph.
