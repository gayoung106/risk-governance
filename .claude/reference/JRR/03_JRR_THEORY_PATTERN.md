
# 03_JRR_THEORY_PATTERN.md

Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Identify how JRR develops theory, derives hypotheses, and justifies causal mechanisms.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Theory Section

Unlike many public administration journals, JRR does not treat theory as a literature review.

The Theory section has one primary purpose:

> To explain **why** the proposed relationships should exist and under what conditions they are expected to emerge.

Accordingly, theory is not descriptive but explanatory.

---

# 2. Canonical Theory Structure

The dominant structure observed across the reviewed papers is:

```

Core phenomenon

↓

Primary theoretical explanation

↓

Alternative or competing explanation

↓

Integration

↓

Research proposition / hypothesis

```

Theory is developed progressively rather than presented as isolated summaries of prior work.

---

# 3. Theory Construction Strategy

Three recurring strategies appear.

---

## Strategy A. Single-Theory Explanation

Structure

```

Theory

↓

Mechanism

↓

Hypothesis

```

Used when:

- relationships are well established
- theoretical consensus exists
- causal direction is relatively uncontested

Observed mainly in:

- trust
- risk perception
- communication studies

---

## Strategy B. Competing Expectations

Most characteristic of JRR.

Structure

```

Theory A predicts X

↓

Theory B predicts Y

↓

Empirical examination

```

Purpose

Not to demonstrate one theory is correct,

but to determine

under which conditions

each explanation better accounts for observed behaviour.

This pattern appears repeatedly in papers dealing with:

- AI
- uncertainty
- public trust
- disaster communication
- misinformation

---

## Strategy C. Integrated Framework

Instead of replacing one theory,

authors integrate multiple theories.

Structure

```

Theory A

+

Theory B

↓

Integrated conceptual framework

↓

Hypotheses

```

Integration is justified by complementary explanatory scope.

---

# 4. Characteristics of Theory Development

JRR papers rarely summarize literature sequentially.

Poor structure:

Theory A

Theory B

Theory C

Theory D

Good structure:

Problem

↓

Mechanism

↓

Supporting theory

↓

Boundary condition

↓

Hypothesis

Theoretical narrative is organised around explanation rather than chronology.

---

# 5. Mechanism Before Variables

A defining characteristic.

Variables are introduced only after explaining the underlying mechanism.

Observed order:

```

Psychological mechanism

↓

Behavioural process

↓

Observable variables

```

Not:

Variable

↓

Definition

↓

Citation

↓

Next variable

Mechanism always precedes operationalisation.

---

# 6. Boundary Conditions

Boundary conditions appear frequently.

Common forms:

risk level

uncertainty

experience

institutional trust

communication source

technology type

social context

Rather than assuming universal effects,

JRR commonly asks:

"When does this relationship become stronger or weaker?"

---

# 7. Moderation Logic

Moderation is almost always theory-driven.

Observed pattern:

Main mechanism

↓

Why another variable changes the mechanism

↓

Interaction hypothesis

Important:

The moderator is not introduced statistically.

It is introduced theoretically.

---

# 8. Mediation Logic

When mediation is used,

authors explain

how

the independent variable changes an internal state,

which then influences behaviour.

Typical chain:

Risk Communication

↓

Trust

↓

Risk Perception

↓

Behaviour

The mediator represents an explanatory process rather than merely an intermediate variable.

---

# 9. Competing Expectations

One of the strongest recurring characteristics.

Observed pattern:

```

Expectation A

↓

Expectation B

↓

The empirical relationship remains uncertain

↓

Research question

↓

Hypothesis

```

Authors rarely state

"The effect will definitely be positive."

Instead,

they acknowledge theoretical tension before presenting hypotheses.

This creates stronger justification for empirical testing.

---

# 10. Hypothesis Writing Style

Typical structure:

Based on the above arguments,

...

Therefore,

...

We expect...

Hypotheses are concise.

Usually one paragraph.

Avoid extensive interpretation.

---

# 11. Number of Theories

Most papers rely on

1–3 core theories.

More than four theories are uncommon.

Excessive theoretical breadth is avoided.

Priority is given to coherence rather than comprehensiveness.

---

# 12. Citation Strategy

Observed characteristics:

- foundational citations for theory
- recent citations for application
- balanced mix of classic and contemporary work

Theory sections avoid unnecessary citation accumulation.

Each citation supports a specific theoretical claim.

---

# 13. Language Characteristics

Frequently used expressions

may explain

helps explain

provides a useful framework

suggests

offers insight into

consistent with

Rarely used

proves

demonstrates beyond doubt

confirms unquestionably

Theory is framed probabilistically rather than deterministically.

---

# 14. Transition to Hypotheses

Observed pattern:

```

Mechanism

↓

Supporting evidence

↓

Remaining uncertainty

↓

Hypothesis

```

Not

Theory

↓

Immediate hypothesis

A short transition paragraph is commonly used to bridge theory and hypotheses.

---

# 15. Conceptual Figure

Many JRR papers include a conceptual framework before the methods section.

Typical elements:

Independent variables

↓

Mediator / Moderator

↓

Dependent variable

↓

Boundary conditions

Figures remain simple.

Visual complexity is avoided.

---

# 16. Editor Notes

Characteristics favoured by JRR editors:

✓ Theory explains mechanisms.

✓ Alternative explanations are acknowledged.

✓ Boundary conditions are specified.

✓ Hypotheses follow logically.

✓ Competing expectations are acceptable when theoretically justified.

✓ Integration is preferred over excessive theoretical expansion.

---

# 17. Common Reviewer Criticisms

Frequently anticipated concerns include:

- Theory is descriptive rather than explanatory.
- Variables are introduced before mechanisms.
- Moderator lacks theoretical justification.
- Hypotheses repeat literature instead of extending it.
- Competing explanations are ignored.
- Boundary conditions are unspecified.

---

# 18. Implications for Our Manuscript

Current strengths

✓ Competing expectation framework.

✓ Mechanism-oriented moderation.

✓ Explicit interaction hypothesis.

Recommended refinements

Reduce descriptive literature summaries.

Emphasise mechanisms before introducing variables.

Clarify why the moderator changes the focal relationship rather than merely interacting statistically.

Present competing explanations before stating hypotheses.

Use one conceptual figure illustrating the proposed moderation framework.

---

# 19. Theory Checklist

□ Does each theory explain a mechanism rather than merely define concepts?

□ Is every hypothesis derived from theoretical reasoning?

□ Are competing explanations acknowledged where appropriate?

□ Is the moderator theoretically justified?

□ Are boundary conditions specified?

□ Are variables introduced only after mechanisms are explained?

□ Does the conceptual framework remain coherent?

□ Are citations used to support arguments rather than accumulate references?

□ Is causal language appropriately restrained?

□ Does the theory naturally transition into hypotheses?

---

# 20. Summary

The defining characteristic of JRR theory sections is not the number of theories employed but the quality of mechanism development.

Successful manuscripts explain:

Why the relationship should exist.

How the relationship operates.

Under what conditions the relationship changes.

These three questions consistently guide theory construction across the reviewed papers.
