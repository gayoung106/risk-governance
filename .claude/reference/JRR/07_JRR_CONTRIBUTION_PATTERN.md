
# 07_JRR_CONTRIBUTION_PATTERN.md

Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Identify how JRR papers articulate contributions and what editors appear to recognise as meaningful scientific novelty.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Contribution Section

One of the strongest patterns across the reviewed JRR papers is that **contribution is never treated as a separate claim detached from the results**.

Instead,

Contribution answers one question:

> What new scientific understanding does this study provide that previous research could not?

Contribution is therefore evidence-based rather than aspirational.

---

# 2. Canonical Contribution Structure

Across the reviewed papers, contribution follows a consistent hierarchy.

```
Empirical finding

↓

Scientific interpretation

↓

Theoretical contribution

↓

Practical implication
```

Contribution is not presented before empirical evidence.

---

# 3. Three Types of Contribution

Nearly every JRR article contains three contribution domains.

---

## A. Theoretical Contribution

Most common.

Examples include

- extending an existing theory
- integrating competing theories
- identifying boundary conditions
- refining causal mechanisms
- explaining inconsistent previous findings

Typical expressions

> This study extends...

> These findings refine...

> Our results suggest that...

---

## B. Methodological Contribution

Less common than theoretical contribution.

Examples

- new measurement strategy
- new analytical framework
- experimental design
- comparative design
- longitudinal evidence
- cross-national evidence

Methodological novelty alone is rarely sufficient.

---

## C. Practical Contribution

Always connected to empirical findings.

Examples

- improve risk communication
- strengthen institutional trust
- redesign warning systems
- improve public engagement
- improve policy implementation

Recommendations remain proportional to evidence.

---

# 4. What JRR Does NOT Consider a Contribution

Observed repeatedly.

The following are rarely presented as contributions by themselves.

❌ First Korean study

❌ First application of a theory

❌ Largest sample

❌ New dataset

❌ Statistical significance

These may strengthen a manuscript,

but they are not treated as the primary contribution.

---

# 5. Mechanism-Based Contribution

Perhaps the strongest recurring characteristic.

Contribution usually concerns

mechanisms,

not relationships.

Weak contribution

```
Trust predicts risk perception.
```

Strong contribution

```
Trust changes how citizens interpret uncertain risk information.
```

The difference is the explanation,

not the coefficient.

---

# 6. Boundary Condition Contribution

Frequently observed.

Instead of asking

"What is the effect?"

JRR papers often ask

"When does the effect change?"

Boundary conditions include

- uncertainty
- institutional trust
- political ideology
- communication source
- experience
- technology

This is one of the most common forms of theoretical novelty.

---

# 7. Integration Contribution

Many papers contribute by integrating

two previously separate research traditions.

Examples observed include

Risk Communication

Trust

AI

Disaster Communication

Emotion

Risk Perception

Institution

Public Behaviour

Integration is valued because it produces a new explanatory framework.

---

# 8. Clarification Contribution

Another recurring pattern.

Contribution is sometimes framed as

clarifying inconsistent findings.

Typical wording

Previous studies have reported mixed findings...

Our findings help explain...

This framing is particularly common when literature contains conflicting evidence.

---

# 9. Context Contribution

Context matters,

but context alone is insufficient.

Weak framing

```
This study examines Korea.
```

Preferred framing

```
This study examines whether the mechanism identified in previous work also operates under different institutional conditions.
```

The contribution is theoretical,

not geographical.

---

# 10. Practical Contribution Pattern

Observed structure

```
Finding

↓

Mechanism

↓

Policy implication
```

Policy recommendations are not speculative.

They emerge directly from empirical findings.

---

# 11. Typical Contribution Language

Frequently observed

extend

refine

advance

clarify

provide evidence

identify

improve understanding

Rarely observed

prove

revolutionise

completely change

confirm once and for all

Contribution language remains cautious.

---

# 12. Placement

Contribution is introduced

twice.

First

Introduction

↓

promise

Second

Discussion

↓

delivery

This symmetry is remarkably consistent.

---

# 13. Contribution Density

Most papers present

3–5

major contributions.

Rarely more.

Attempting to claim excessive novelty appears uncommon.

---

# 14. Editor Perspective

Editors appear to favour manuscripts that answer

```
Why do we understand this phenomenon better now?
```

rather than

```
What new variable was added?
```

Mechanistic insight outweighs variable novelty.

---

# 15. Common Reviewer Criticisms

Likely revision comments

"The contribution is incremental."

"The novelty is unclear."

"The manuscript reports relationships but not mechanisms."

"The practical implications exceed the evidence."

"The discussion repeats the results without identifying theoretical advancement."

---

# 16. Contribution Formula

Across the reviewed papers,

the strongest contributions follow this template.

```
Previous research explained A.

↓

This study explains why A changes under condition B.

↓

This extends theoretical understanding of mechanism C.

↓

These findings have implications for practice D.
```

This formula appears repeatedly,

even though wording differs.

---

# 17. Implications for Our Manuscript

Current strengths

✓ Mechanism-oriented moderation.

✓ Boundary condition framework.

✓ Competing expectations.

Potential theoretical contribution

Instead of

```
Organisational factors affect policy expectation.
```

frame as

```
The influence of organisational conditions depends on individuals' capability to interpret and utilise data, revealing that policy expectations emerge through conditional organisational processes rather than organisational resources alone.
```

This shifts contribution

from relationship

to mechanism.

---

# 18. Practical Contribution for Our Study

Avoid

```
Government should improve organisational support.
```

Prefer

```
Because organisational support operates differently across capability levels, policy interventions should distinguish between capability development and organisational resource provision rather than assuming identical effects across employees.
```

Specificity increases credibility.

---

# 19. Contribution Checklist

□ Is the contribution theoretical rather than descriptive?

□ Does it explain a mechanism?

□ Does it identify a boundary condition?

□ Does it refine existing theory?

□ Does it integrate separate research streams?

□ Does it explain previous inconsistencies?

□ Are practical implications supported by findings?

□ Are contributions revisited in the Discussion?

□ Does the manuscript avoid claiming novelty based solely on context or dataset?

□ Would the contribution remain meaningful if conducted in another country?

---

# 20. Summary

The defining characteristic of JRR contributions is that they are **mechanism-driven rather than result-driven**.

Successful papers do not simply report that variables are associated.

Instead, they demonstrate:

- why those associations emerge,
- under what conditions they change,
- how they refine existing theoretical understanding,
- and what evidence-based implications follow.

This emphasis on explanatory contribution consistently distinguishes high-quality JRR publications from manuscripts that merely report statistically significant findings.
