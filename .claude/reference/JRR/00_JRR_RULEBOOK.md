
# JRR RULEBOOK

Version 1.0

Purpose

This document defines the mandatory writing rules for all manuscripts
intended for submission to the Journal of Risk Research (JRR).

All subsequent drafting, editing, reviewing, and rewriting should follow
these rules unless there is a compelling reason not to.

---

# PART I

## Research Question

Rule 1

Research begins with a societal risk problem.

Never begin with technology.

---

Rule 2

Research questions must explain

WHY

or

WHEN,

not merely WHETHER.

---

Rule 3

Every research question should remain meaningful outside one country.

---

Rule 4

The manuscript must answer a scientific question,

not merely a policy question.

---

Rule 5

Research questions should identify uncertainty.

Avoid questions whose answers are already obvious.

---

# PART II

## Introduction

Rule 6

Always begin with the societal problem.

Never begin with theory.

---

Rule 7

Technology is context.

Risk is the problem.

---

Rule 8

Present the literature selectively.

Do not summarize everything.

---

Rule 9

The gap must be scientific,

not geographic.

---

Rule 10

The contribution should be promised in the Introduction,

not fully explained.

---

Rule 11

Paper organization belongs only at the end of the Introduction.

---

# PART III

## Theory

Rule 12

Mechanisms before variables.

---

Rule 13

Theory explains why.

Variables describe what.

---

Rule 14

Every hypothesis must follow a mechanism.

---

Rule 15

Competing explanations strengthen theory.

Do not hide alternative explanations.

---

Rule 16

Boundary conditions increase contribution.

---

Rule 17

One coherent theory is stronger than five disconnected theories.

---

# PART IV

## Constructs

Rule 18

Construct labels must exactly match questionnaire items.

---

Rule 19

Never broaden a construct beyond its measurement.

---

Rule 20

Definition

↓

Measurement

↓

Evidence

must always appear together.

---

Rule 21

Reverse coding must always be explicitly reported.

---

Rule 22

Construct validity is never assumed.

Always justify it.

---

# PART V

## Methods

Rule 23

Transparency is more important than complexity.

---

Rule 24

State

Design

↓

Sample

↓

Measures

↓

Analysis

in this order.

---

Rule 25

Every analytical decision requires justification.

---

Rule 26

Robustness analyses strengthen credibility.

---

Rule 27

Report software versions.

---

Rule 28

Discuss Common Method Bias whenever survey data are used.

---

# PART VI

## Results

Rule 29

Results report.

Discussion interprets.

Never mix them.

---

Rule 30

Present models sequentially.

---

Rule 31

Report unsupported hypotheses honestly.

---

Rule 32

Interaction requires simple slopes.

---

Rule 33

Tables should never repeat the text.

---

# PART VII

## Discussion

Rule 34

Begin with substantive findings,

not coefficients.

---

Rule 35

Explain mechanisms,

not statistics.

---

Rule 36

Unexpected findings deserve explanation,

not omission.

---

Rule 37

Contribution precedes implication.

---

Rule 38

Policy implications must follow evidence.

---

Rule 39

Limitations increase credibility when honestly discussed.

---

# PART VIII

## Contribution

Rule 40

Contribution is scientific,

not contextual.

---

Rule 41

Mechanism is more valuable than another variable.

---

Rule 42

Boundary conditions create theoretical novelty.

---

Rule 43

Theory refinement is stronger than theory confirmation.

---

Rule 44

Never claim novelty solely because the study was conducted in Korea.

---

# PART IX

## Writing

Rule 45

Use restrained scientific language.

---

Rule 46

Avoid promotional adjectives.

---

Rule 47

Every paragraph should answer one question only.

---

Rule 48

Transitions should explain logical progression.

---

Rule 49

Delete repetition.

---

Rule 50

Every sentence should support the research question.

Otherwise,

delete or rewrite it.

---

# PART X

## Reviewer Perspective

Rule 51

Assume reviewers distrust every unsupported claim.

Provide evidence.

---

Rule 52

If a reviewer can ask

"How do you know?"

the manuscript needs revision.

---

Rule 53

If construct validity is questionable,

fix it before running new analyses.

---

Rule 54

Statistical sophistication never compensates for weak theory.

---

Rule 55

Theory always wins over complexity.

---

Rule 56

Editors publish explanations,

not coefficients.

---

Rule 57

Risk should remain the central analytical object.

Technology should remain contextual.

---

Rule 58

Never oversell contribution.

---

Rule 59

Recommendations must be proportional to evidence.

---

Rule 60

A manuscript is ready only when every section strengthens the same scientific story.

---

# FINAL COMMAND FOR AI

When revising any manuscript:

Identify violations of these rules.

Classify each violation as

Critical

Major

Minor.

Revise the manuscript while preserving scientific meaning.

Prefer improving

Mechanism

Gap

Construct Validity

Contribution

before editing language.

Do not increase manuscript length unless necessary.

Always explain why each revision improves compliance with the JRR Rulebook.
