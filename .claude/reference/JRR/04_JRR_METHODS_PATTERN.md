
# 04_JRR_METHODS_PATTERN.md

Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Identify the methodological reporting standards and reviewer expectations observed in Journal of Risk Research.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Methods Section

The Methods section in JRR is not intended to demonstrate statistical sophistication.

Its primary objective is to convince readers that the empirical evidence is sufficiently rigorous to answer the stated research question.

Accordingly, methodological transparency is consistently prioritised over methodological complexity.

---

# 2. Canonical Methods Structure

Across the reviewed papers, the dominant structure is:

```

Research Design

↓

Data Collection

↓

Sample

↓

Measures

↓

Analytical Strategy

↓

Robustness / Additional Analysis

```

The order is remarkably consistent regardless of analytical technique.

---

# 3. Research Design

The first subsection almost always clarifies:

- observational or experimental
- cross-sectional or longitudinal
- survey or behavioural experiment
- quantitative or mixed-method

Typical wording:

> This study employed...

> We conducted...

> Data were collected through...

The design is explicitly stated before discussing respondents.

---

# 4. Data Collection

JRR places considerable emphasis on transparency.

Typical reporting includes:

- survey period
- survey platform
- recruitment procedure
- inclusion criteria
- exclusion criteria
- ethical approval (if applicable)

Not every article reports all elements, but omission of recruitment procedures is uncommon.

---

# 5. Sample Reporting

Common elements:

Sample size

Country

Population

Response rate

Mean age

Gender distribution

Relevant contextual characteristics

Where appropriate:

education

occupation

experience

institution

risk exposure

---

# 6. Missing Data

Most papers explicitly report:

- exclusion criteria
- missing observations
- final analytical sample

Rather than silently deleting observations.

Transparency is preferred.

---

# 7. Measurement Reporting

The measures subsection follows a highly consistent pattern.

Each construct typically includes:

Definition

↓

Operationalisation

↓

Example items

↓

Scale

↓

Reliability

Example structure:

Construct definition

Example survey items

5- or 7-point Likert scale

Cronbach's alpha

References

Importantly,

complete questionnaires are rarely reproduced in the manuscript itself.

Representative items are sufficient.

---

# 8. Construct Validity

JRR expects authors to explain

why

their operationalisation reflects the theoretical construct.

Common reviewer concern:

Variable labels do not match actual questionnaire items.

Accordingly,

construct definitions should precede measurement descriptions.

---

# 9. Reliability

Cronbach's α remains the dominant reliability statistic.

Frequently reported:

α

Composite Reliability (occasionally)

McDonald's ω (rare)

Thresholds are generally interpreted conservatively.

---

# 10. Validity

Depending on the analytical approach:

Exploratory Factor Analysis

Confirmatory Factor Analysis

Convergent validity

Discriminant validity

Content validity

Not every paper performs CFA.

Choice depends on design.

Authors justify rather than automatically perform advanced validation.

---

# 11. Statistical Analysis

Typical sequence:

Descriptive statistics

↓

Correlation analysis

↓

Primary regression

↓

Additional models

↓

Interaction or mediation

↓

Sensitivity analysis

Readers are guided through increasing analytical complexity.

---

# 12. Regression Reporting

Regression sections generally include:

Model specification

Coefficient estimates

Confidence intervals or standard errors

p-values

Model fit statistics

Assumption checking is discussed when relevant.

---

# 13. Moderation Analysis

Moderation is presented as

theoretical evidence,

not merely statistical interaction.

Typical reporting:

Interaction coefficient

↓

Simple slope analysis

↓

Interaction figure

↓

Interpretation

Johnson–Neyman analysis appears occasionally but is not universal.

---

# 14. Mediation Analysis

Frequently reported using:

bootstrapping

indirect effects

confidence intervals

PROCESS

SEM

The emphasis remains on explaining mechanisms.

---

# 15. Robustness Checks

High-quality JRR papers commonly report additional analyses.

Examples:

Alternative specifications

Alternative measurements

Sensitivity analysis

Subgroup analysis

Alternative model estimation

Purpose:

Demonstrate that conclusions are not model-dependent.

---

# 16. Common Method Bias

Survey-based studies increasingly acknowledge common method bias.

Typical approaches:

Procedural remedies

Harman's single-factor test

Marker variables (less common)

CFA comparison (occasionally)

Even when no formal correction is applied,

authors usually discuss the issue.

---

# 17. Multicollinearity

Frequently reported:

Correlation matrix

VIF

Tolerance

Condition Index (occasionally)

Thresholds are explicitly stated.

---

# 18. Software Reporting

Most papers report:

Statistical software

Version

Relevant packages

Examples:

R

SPSS

Stata

Python

Mplus

AMOS

Reporting software versions improves reproducibility.

# 19. Reproducibility

An emerging trend.

Increasing numbers of papers describe:

data availability

code availability

ethical approval

supplementary materials

Transparency is becoming a publication norm.

---

# 20. Language Characteristics

Preferred wording:

We examined...

We estimated...

We assessed...

We evaluated...

Less preferred:

We proved...

We confirmed...

We established...

Methods describe procedures,

not findings.

---

# 21. Common Reviewer Criticisms

Observed methodological weaknesses likely to trigger revision:

Unclear construct definitions.

Mismatch between questionnaire items and construct labels.

Insufficient explanation of sampling.

Incomplete reporting of exclusion criteria.

Lack of robustness analysis.

Unjustified statistical choices.

Insufficient validity evidence.

Failure to explain moderation theoretically.

---

# 22. Editor Notes

Characteristics consistently favoured by JRR editors:

✓ Transparent sampling.

✓ Clear construct operationalisation.

✓ Appropriate—not excessive—statistical sophistication.

✓ Robustness checks.

✓ Reproducible reporting.

✓ Honest acknowledgement of limitations.

Editors appear to prefer methodological credibility over methodological novelty.

# 24. Methods Checklist

□ Research design explicitly stated.

□ Data collection procedures reported.

□ Sampling process transparent.

□ Inclusion/exclusion criteria reported.

□ Final sample explained.

□ Constructs theoretically defined.

□ Measures accurately reflect questionnaire items.

□ Reliability statistics reported.

□ Validity evidence discussed.

□ Statistical models justified.

□ Robustness checks included.

□ Multicollinearity assessed.

□ Common method bias considered.

□ Software and versions reported.

□ Analytical decisions are reproducible.

---

# 25. Summary

The defining feature of JRR methods sections is methodological transparency.

Successful manuscripts do not attempt to impress readers with statistical complexity.

Instead, they demonstrate that:

- the data appropriately represent the research problem,
- the constructs accurately capture theoretical concepts,
- the analytical strategy is justified,
- and the reported findings are robust and reproducible.

These characteristics consistently distinguish publishable empirical papers in the reviewed JRR corpus.
