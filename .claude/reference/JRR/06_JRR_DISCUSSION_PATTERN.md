# 06_JRR_DISCUSSION_PATTERN.md

Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Identify how Discussion sections are organised, how findings are interpreted, and how theoretical and practical contributions are articulated in JRR.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Discussion

The Discussion section answers a fundamentally different question from the Results section.

Results answer:

> What did we find?

Discussion answers:

> Why do these findings matter?

Accordingly, the Discussion reconnects empirical findings to:

- theory,
- previous literature,
- practical implications,
- limitations,
- future research.

The section is interpretive rather than statistical.

---

# 2. Canonical Discussion Structure

Across the reviewed papers, the dominant structure is:

```

Brief summary of findings

↓

Interpretation using theory

↓

Comparison with previous studies

↓

Theoretical contribution

↓

Practical implication

↓

Limitations

↓

Future research

```

This order appears with remarkable consistency.

---

# 3. Opening Paragraph

The first paragraph never repeats numerical results.

Instead, it summarizes only the central finding.

Typical pattern:

```

Overall,

this study demonstrates...

↓

The findings suggest...

↓

These results extend...

```

The opening paragraph immediately shifts from reporting to interpretation.

---

# 4. Theory First

The most important characteristic.

Discussion almost always begins with theory.

Not policy.

Not recommendations.

Observed structure:

```

Finding

↓

Theoretical interpretation

↓

Scientific meaning

```

Theory remains the primary organising principle.

---

# 5. Relationship with Previous Literature

After interpreting findings,

authors compare them with previous studies.

Possible patterns

### Consistent

Our findings are consistent with...

---

### Extension

Our findings extend...

---

### Contradiction

Contrary to previous work...

---

### Clarification

These findings help explain why...

JRR does not avoid contradictory evidence.

Contradictions are often presented as opportunities for theoretical refinement.

---

# 6. Mechanism Interpretation

One of the strongest recurring patterns.

Discussion rarely says

Variable A affects Variable B.

Instead,

authors explain

WHY

the relationship emerged.

Typical wording

This may reflect...

One possible explanation is...

A plausible interpretation is...

The findings suggest that...

Mechanisms dominate over coefficients.

---

# 7. Unexpected Findings

Unexpected results receive substantial attention.

Rather than ignoring unsupported hypotheses,

authors ask

Why did this occur?

Possible explanations include:

measurement

context

boundary conditions

institutional differences

psychological mechanisms

Unexpected findings become theoretical opportunities.

---

# 8. Theoretical Contribution

Usually introduced explicitly.

Common forms

This study contributes...

These findings extend...

Our results refine...

Contribution is discussed before policy implications.

---

# 9. Practical Contribution

Policy implications are grounded in empirical findings.

Observed order

Finding

↓

Interpretation

↓

Practical recommendation

Recommendations are never detached from evidence.

---

# 10. Policy Implications

JRR papers rarely make broad recommendations.

Instead,

recommendations are specific.

Examples

Improve communication transparency.

Increase institutional trust.

Design warning messages differently.

Reduce uncertainty.

Enhance public engagement.

Policy recommendations remain proportional to empirical evidence.

---

# 11. Language Characteristics

Preferred expressions

may indicate

suggest

appear to

could reflect

may explain

consistent with

Avoid

prove

demonstrate conclusively

clearly establishes

Discussion remains cautious.

---

# 12. Boundary Conditions

Many discussions identify situations where findings may differ.

Examples

Different countries

Different hazards

Different technologies

Different institutional settings

Boundary conditions strengthen theoretical credibility.

---

# 13. Limitation Section

Highly standardised.

Common limitations

Cross-sectional data

Self-report measures

Country-specific context

Sample representativeness

Measurement limitations

No attempt is made to minimise limitations.

Instead,

limitations are acknowledged transparently.

---

# 14. Future Research

Future research follows directly from limitations.

Examples

Longitudinal studies

Experimental designs

Cross-national comparisons

Alternative mechanisms

Different populations

Future directions are realistic rather than speculative.

---

# 15. Discussion Length

Observed tendency

Approximately

25–35%

of the total manuscript.

Long enough to interpret findings,

short enough to avoid repetition.

---

# 16. Common Reviewer Criticisms

Likely revision requests include:

Discussion repeats Results.

Policy implications are unsupported.

Theory is not revisited.

Unexpected findings are ignored.

Contribution is unclear.

Limitations are superficial.

Future research is generic.

---

# 17. Editor Notes

Editors appear to value:

✓ Theory-driven interpretation.

✓ Honest discussion of unexpected findings.

✓ Balanced practical implications.

✓ Explicit theoretical contribution.

✓ Transparent limitations.

Overstated claims are consistently avoided.

---

# 18. Discussion Flow

Observed pattern

```

Main finding

↓

Mechanism

↓

Comparison with literature

↓

Contribution

↓

Policy implication

↓

Limitations

↓

Future research

```

The flow is cumulative.

Each subsection builds on the previous one.

---

# 19. Discussion Writing Rules

Rule 1

Interpret.

Do not report.

---

Rule 2

Explain mechanisms.

Not coefficients.

---

Rule 3

Connect findings to theory.

Not merely to previous studies.

---

Rule 4

State contribution explicitly.

Never assume readers will infer it.

---

Rule 5

Recommendations must emerge from findings.

---

Rule 6

Acknowledge limitations honestly.

---

# 20. Implications for Our Manuscript

Current strengths

✓ Mechanism-oriented framing.

✓ Competing theoretical explanations.

✓ Moderation provides boundary conditions.

Recommended improvements

Open Discussion with one paragraph summarising only the principal findings.

---

Interpret the negative coefficients through theoretical mechanisms rather than statistical language.

---

Separate

Theoretical Contribution

and

Policy Implication

into independent subsections.

---

Discuss unsupported or weak hypotheses explicitly.

Do not ignore them.

---

State limitations in relation to

measurement,

cross-sectional design,

and

generalisability.

---

Future research should extend naturally from identified limitations.

---

# 21. Discussion Checklist

□ Opens with substantive findings.

□ Results are interpreted, not repeated.

□ Theory is revisited.

□ Previous literature is compared.

□ Unexpected findings are explained.

□ Mechanisms are discussed.

□ Contributions are explicit.

□ Policy implications are evidence-based.

□ Limitations are transparent.

□ Future research is realistic.

□ Causal language remains restrained.

---

# 22. Summary

The defining characteristic of JRR Discussion sections is theoretical interpretation.

Successful papers move beyond reporting statistical significance.

Instead, they answer four questions:

What did we learn?

Why did it occur?

Why does it matter theoretically?

How should practice change?

Maintaining this progression consistently distinguishes high-quality Discussion sections in the reviewed JRR corpus.
