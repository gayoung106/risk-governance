
# 09_JRR_REVIEWER_CHECKLIST.md

Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Reconstruct the implicit review criteria used by JRR reviewers and provide a practical pre-submission checklist.

---

# Overview

This checklist is designed to simulate a typical JRR reviewer.

Instead of asking

> "Is this manuscript good?"

the reviewer repeatedly asks

> "Can I recommend publication without asking the authors to rewrite major parts of this paper?"

Accordingly, every item below should ideally be answered **YES** before submission.

---

# SECTION 1

## Research Question

### Reviewer Question 1

Is the research question explicitly stated?

□ Yes

□ No

---

### Reviewer Question 2

Is the question scientifically meaningful rather than merely descriptive?

Good

> Why do citizens trust AI-generated warnings differently?

Poor

> What do citizens think about AI?

---

### Reviewer Question 3

Does the research question emerge naturally from the literature?

YES / NO

---

### Reviewer Question 4

Would the question still matter if the study were conducted in another country?

YES / NO

If NO,

the question is probably context-specific rather than theoretically meaningful.

# SECTION 2

## Literature Review

Reviewer expectations

□ Literature is selective.

□ Literature supports arguments.

□ Literature is not a historical summary.

□ Literature directly motivates the gap.

□ Literature contains recent studies.

□ Literature includes foundational theory.

---

Common reviewer comment

> "The literature review is descriptive."

Risk Level

★★★★★

---

# SECTION 3

## Research Gap

Reviewer asks

Why is another study necessary?

Checklist

□ Gap is scientific.

□ Gap is theoretically important.

□ Gap is not simply geographic.

□ Gap identifies uncertainty.

□ Gap is specific.

---

Weak Gap

"There is little Korean research."

Strong Gap

"Previous studies explain trust but not the mechanism through which uncertainty alters trust."

---

# SECTION 4

## Theory

Reviewer expectations

□ Theory explains mechanisms.

□ Constructs emerge naturally.

□ Competing explanations acknowledged.

□ Boundary conditions identified.

□ Hypotheses logically derived.

□ Theory is internally coherent.

---

Major Revision trigger

Variables appear before theory.

---

# SECTION 5

## Constructs

Reviewer questions

Does every construct have

Definition

↓

Operationalisation

↓

Measurement

↓

Evidence

?

Checklist

□ Construct labels match questionnaire items.

□ Construct definitions are theoretically justified.

□ Operationalisation reflects theory.

□ Measurement corresponds to the construct.

□ No construct contamination.

---

Major Revision trigger

Construct labels inconsistent with questionnaire.

Risk Level

★★★★★

# SECTION 6

## Measurement

Reviewer checks

□ Scale reported.

□ Example items reported.

□ Reliability reported.

□ Validity discussed.

□ Reverse coding explained.

□ Composite variables justified.

---

Common reviewer request

"Please clarify how this construct was measured."

Who participated?

Why were they appropriate?

Checklist

□ Sampling explained.

□ Response screening described.

□ Missing data addressed.

## Discussion

Reviewer expectations

□ Findings interpreted theoretically.

□ Previous studies revisited.

□ Unexpected findings discussed.

□ Boundary conditions explained.

□ Contribution explicitly stated.

# SECTION 11

## Contribution

Reviewer asks

After reading this paper,

what do we know that we did not know before?

Checklist

□ Contribution is theoretical.

□ Contribution is mechanism-based.

□ Contribution extends knowledge.

□ Contribution is explicit.

□ Contribution is proportional.

---

Weak

"This is the first Korean study."

Strong

"This study identifies a previously unrecognised mechanism linking institutional trust and AI-assisted risk interpretation."

Recommendations should

follow directly

from

empirical findings.

Checklist

□ Recommendations are realistic.

□ Recommendations are evidence-based.

# SECTION 13

## Limitations

Reviewer questions

Did the authors acknowledge

the real limitations?

Checklist

□ Cross-sectional design discussed.

□ Measurement limitations discussed.

□ Sampling limitations discussed.

□ Generalisability discussed.

□ Future research follows logically.

---

Weak

"This study has several limitations."

Strong

"The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and future longitudinal research is needed to examine temporal dynamics."

# Final Remark

The reviewed JRR corpus suggests that reviewers consistently prioritise:

1. A meaningful scientific question.
2. A defensible theoretical mechanism.
3. Valid construct measurement.
4. Transparent empirical methods.
5. A clear contribution to risk research.

A manuscript that performs well on these five dimensions is substantially more likely to progress successfully through peer review than one relying primarily on analytical complexity or statistical novelty.
