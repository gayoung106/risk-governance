
# 05_JRR_RESULTS_PATTERN.md

Version 1.0
Source: Uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles only.
Purpose: Identify how empirical findings are presented, interpreted, and organised in Journal of Risk Research.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Results Section

The Results section in JRR serves one purpose:

> **Present empirical evidence objectively before interpretation.**

Interpretation belongs primarily in the Discussion.

Accordingly,

Results sections remain remarkably restrained.

---

# 2. Canonical Results Structure

Across the reviewed papers, the dominant sequence is:

```

Sample description

↓

Descriptive statistics

↓

Correlation (if applicable)

↓

Primary model

↓

Additional model(s)

↓

Interaction / Mediation

↓

Robustness analysis

↓

Brief summary

```

Readers are gradually guided from simple evidence to more complex analyses.

---

# 3. Descriptive Statistics

Nearly every empirical paper begins with sample characteristics.

Typical contents

- Mean
- Standard deviation
- Minimum
- Maximum

Often accompanied by

Table 1

No interpretation.

Only reporting.

---

# 4. Correlation Matrix

Frequently reported before regression.

Purpose:

- preliminary relationships
- multicollinearity inspection

Usually accompanied by

Cronbach's α

or

construct reliability.

Interpretation is limited.

Example:

"The variables were moderately correlated."

Nothing more.

---

# 5. Main Regression Models

Typical sequence

Model 1

↓

Model 2

↓

Model 3

↓

Full Model

Each model introduces additional variables.

For example

Model 1

Controls

↓

Model 2

Main predictors

↓

Model 3

Interaction

This incremental modelling strategy is common.

---

# 6. Reporting Regression Results

Standard reporting includes

β

SE

95% CI (occasionally)

p

R²

ΔR²

F-statistic

Model comparisons are preferred over presenting only the final model.

---

# 7. Interpretation Style

Observed style

Good

"The coefficient was negative and statistically significant."

Poor

"This proves..."

Results describe.

They do not explain.

---

# 8. Moderation Reporting

Typical sequence

Interaction coefficient

↓

Simple slopes

↓

Interaction figure

↓

Short interpretation

Theoretical discussion is intentionally postponed.

Example wording

"The interaction term was statistically significant."

"The relationship became weaker at higher levels of..."

No extensive interpretation.

---

# 9. Simple Slope Analysis

Common reporting pattern

Low moderator

↓

Mean

↓

High moderator

Accompanied by

Figure

Occasionally

Johnson–Neyman region

Simple slopes are described numerically.

Not theoretically.

---

# 10. Mediation Reporting

Typical order

Indirect effect

↓

Bootstrapped confidence interval

↓

Direct effect

↓

Total effect

Authors avoid discussing implications here.

---

# 11. Figures

Figures appear only when they improve understanding.

Most common

Interaction plot

Prediction plot

Risk perception curves

Probability curves

Visualisation remains simple.

No decorative graphics.

---

# 12. Tables

Typical tables

Table 1

Sample characteristics

Table 2

Descriptive statistics

Table 3

Regression

Table 4

Additional analyses

Each table has a distinct purpose.

Redundancy is avoided.

---

# 13. Robustness Analysis

Frequently included.

Examples

Alternative specification

Alternative estimator

Alternative dependent variable

Subgroup analysis

Sensitivity analysis

Robustness appears after the primary analysis.

Never before.

---

# 14. Negative Findings

Important observation.

JRR publishes

non-significant findings.

Authors simply report them.

Example style

"Hypothesis 3 was not supported."

No attempt to reinterpret the result immediately.

---

# 15. Language Characteristics

Frequently observed verbs

found

observed

estimated

identified

associated

Rarely observed

proved

demonstrated conclusively

confirmed without doubt

Objectivity dominates.

---

# 16. Common Reviewer Criticisms

Likely revision triggers

Mixing interpretation with reporting.

Repeating tables in text.

Ignoring non-significant findings.

Reporting p-values without effect sizes.

Presenting figures that duplicate tables.

Insufficient explanation of interaction analyses.

---

# 17. Editor Notes

Characteristics consistently favoured

✓ Logical order

✓ Clear tables

✓ Limited interpretation

✓ Honest reporting

✓ Appropriate robustness

Editors appear to value clarity more than analytical complexity.

---

# 18. Writing Rules

Results should answer

"What did the analysis show?"

NOT

"What does it mean?"

Meaning belongs later.

---

# 19. Typical Paragraph Structure

Each model is presented using

Model introduction

↓

Numerical results

↓

Support / not support

↓

Transition

This pattern repeats throughout the section.

---

# 20. Statistical Reporting Style

Preferred

β = -.24, p < .01

The interaction coefficient was positive.

The effect remained significant.

Avoid

Large effects

Strong influence

Important relationship

Those are discussion-level statements.

---

# 21. How JRR Reports Hypotheses

Typical wording

"H1 was supported."

"H2 received partial support."

"H3 was not supported."

No lengthy explanation.

Usually one sentence.

---

# 22. Transition to Discussion

Results conclude with a brief summary.

Example pattern

Overall,

the findings indicate...

No theoretical implications yet.

Discussion begins on a new page or section.

---

Current strengths

✓ Johnson–Neyman analysis

Recommended improvements

Separate

Results

from

Discussion

completely.

Do not explain

WHY

coefficients appeared.

Only describe

WHAT

appeared.

Report

β

SE

p

95% CI

R²

consistently.

---

State

Supported

Partially supported

Not supported

after every hypothesis.

---

Include one interaction figure only.

---

---

# 24. Results Checklist

□ Sample characteristics reported.

□ Descriptive statistics presented.

□ Correlations reported.

□ Models presented sequentially.

□ Regression coefficients complete.

□ Effect sizes included.

□ Interaction analysed appropriately.

□ Figures add information.

□ Robustness analyses reported.

□ Interpretation postponed.

□ Every hypothesis evaluated.

□ Results conclude with a concise summary.

---

# 25. Summary

The defining characteristic of JRR Results sections is disciplined reporting.

Authors distinguish clearly between:

Evidence

and

Interpretation.

The Results section establishes empirical credibility.

The Discussion section establishes scientific meaning.

Maintaining this separation is one of the strongest stylistic characteristics observed across the reviewed JRR corpus.
