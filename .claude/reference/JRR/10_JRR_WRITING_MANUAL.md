
# 10_JRR_WRITING_MANUAL.md

Version 1.0
Source: Reverse-engineered from the uploaded Journal of Risk Research (JRR) articles.

Purpose

This document serves as a complete writing manual for preparing empirical manuscripts for the *Journal of Risk Research*.

Unlike the previous documents, this manual integrates the findings from the analyses of Introduction, Theory, Methods, Results, Discussion, editorial preferences, and reviewer expectations into one practical workflow.

---

# PART I.

## Before Writing

The first question is NOT

> What data do I have?

Instead ask

> What scientific uncertainty about risk can this study resolve?

This distinction characterises almost every successful JRR article.

A publishable manuscript starts from an unanswered scientific problem rather than from an available dataset.

---

# PART II.

## Selecting the Research Question

A suitable JRR question usually satisfies four conditions.

### Condition 1

The question concerns

- uncertainty
- risk
- trust
- communication
- governance
- perception
- behavioural response
- institutional decision making

---

### Condition 2

The question asks

WHY

or

WHEN

rather than

WHETHER.

Weak

> Does trust influence risk perception?

Strong

> Under what institutional conditions does trust alter risk perception?

---

### Condition 3

The question identifies

a mechanism.

---

### Condition 4

The answer has implications beyond one country.

## Constructing the Introduction

Recommended paragraph structure

### Paragraph 1

---

### Paragraph 2

---

### Paragraph 3

---

### Paragraph 4

Scientific uncertainty.

---

### Paragraph 5

Research gap.

---

### Paragraph 6

Research objective.

---

### Paragraph 7

Contribution.

---

### Paragraph 8

Paper organisation.

---

Never reverse this order.

# PART IV.

## Writing the Literature Review

The literature review is

NOT

a history of previous studies.

Instead,

every cited study should answer one question.

Why is this citation necessary?

If a citation does not support

- the problem,
- the gap,
- the theory,
- or the hypothesis,

remove it.

---

# PART V.

## Building Theory

Theory should answer

Why should this relationship exist?

A complete theory section usually follows

Mechanism

↓

Supporting theory

↓

Boundary condition

↓

Hypothesis

Do not organise the theory section by author names.

Organise it by explanatory logic.

---

# PART VI.

## Hypothesis Development

Every hypothesis should satisfy

Theory

↓

Mechanism

↓

Empirical expectation

↓

Hypothesis

Avoid

Literature

↓

Hypothesis

without mechanism.

---

# PART VII.

## Research Design

State

- design
- data
- sample
- recruitment
- period
- ethics

before discussing variables.

Transparency is more important than complexity.

---

# PART VIII.

## Variables

For every construct report

Definition

↓

Measurement

↓

Scale

↓

Reliability

↓

Supporting reference

Never label a construct more broadly than its questionnaire items justify.

Construct validity is one of the strongest reviewer concerns.

---

# PART IX.

## Statistical Analysis

JRR does not reward complexity for its own sake.

Preferred order

Descriptive

↓

Correlation

↓

Primary model

↓

Additional model

↓

Interaction

↓

Robustness

Readers should always understand

why

each analysis is performed.

---

# PART X.

## Reporting Results

Results answer

What happened?

Discussion answers

Why did it happen?

Never mix the two.

---

Each model should follow

Model introduction

↓

Coefficient

↓

Support / Not support

↓

Transition

Avoid theoretical interpretation here.

---

# PART XI.

## Writing the Discussion

The Discussion should revisit

Theory,

not

Tables.

Recommended structure

Summary

↓

Mechanism

↓

Comparison

↓

Contribution

↓

Policy implication

↓

Limitations

↓

Future research

---

# PART XII.

## Writing Contributions

Every contribution should answer

What new scientific understanding emerges?

Not

What variables were analysed?

Contribution should involve

- mechanism,
- theory,
- or boundary conditions.

Dataset novelty alone is insufficient.

---

# PART XIII.

## Writing Policy Implications

Every recommendation must satisfy

Finding

↓

Mechanism

↓

Recommendation

Avoid

general statements.

Instead write

Because...

therefore...

This improves scientific credibility.

---

# PART XIV.

## Writing Limitations

Limitations should include

Design

Measurement

Sampling

Generalisability

Potential omitted variables

Future research should directly address those limitations.

# PART XV.

## Writing Style

Preferred style

objective

precise

moderate

analytical

Avoid

marketing language,

emotional language,

or exaggerated claims.

# PART XVI.

## Common Writing Mistakes

### Mistake 1

Technology-first introduction.

Correct

Problem-first introduction.

---

### Mistake 2

Geographical gap.

Correct

Scientific gap.

---

### Mistake 3

Theory as literature summary.

Correct

Theory as mechanism.

---

### Mistake 4

Variables before concepts.

Correct

Concepts before variables.

---

### Mistake 5

Discussion repeating Results.

Correct

Discussion interpreting Results.

---

### Mistake 6

Policy implications unsupported by evidence.

Correct

Evidence-driven recommendations.

---

### Mistake 7

Construct labels broader than questionnaire items.

Correct

Measurement-driven construct definitions.

---

# PART XVII.

## Self-Editing Workflow

Step 1

Read only the Introduction.

Can the research question be identified?

---

Step 2

Read only the Theory.

Can every hypothesis be justified?

---

Step 3

Read only the Methods.

Could another researcher reproduce the study?

---

Step 4

Read only the Results.

Are findings understandable without interpretation?

---

Step 5

Read only the Discussion.

Does it explain mechanisms rather than coefficients?

---

Step 6

Read only the Contribution.

Does the manuscript genuinely advance knowledge?

# PART XIX.

## Final Submission Checklist

Before submission

confirm

□ The introduction begins with a societal risk problem.

□ The gap is scientific.

□ The theory explains mechanisms.

□ Constructs match measurements.

□ Methods are transparent.

□ Results are objective.

□ Discussion interprets.

□ Contributions are explicit.

□ Policy implications are evidence-based.

□ Limitations are honest.

□ Future research follows naturally.

□ Writing is concise.

# PART XX.

## Core Editorial Principle

Across the reviewed JRR corpus,

the single most consistent publication principle is the following:

> **A publishable JRR paper does not merely demonstrate that relationships exist; it explains why they emerge, under what conditions they change, and why that new understanding improves the science of risk.**

Every section of the manuscript should reinforce this principle.

If a paragraph cannot be linked to this objective, it should be revised or removed.

---

# One-Sentence Writing Rule

When in doubt, ask:

**"Does this paragraph improve scientific understanding of risk, or is it merely describing my study?"**

If it is only describing the study,

rewrite it.

This single question captures the dominant writing philosophy observed across the reviewed Journal of Risk Research articles.
