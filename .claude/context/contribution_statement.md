# Contribution Statement

## Core Contribution

The manuscript argues that:

post-regulatory governance systems may structurally fail to generate public trust under risk society conditions.

This limitation becomes particularly salient in disaster-related personal data governance.

---

## Theoretical Contribution

This project contributes theoretically by:

1. Connecting risk society theory with privacy governance
2. Explaining trust formation institutionally rather than merely psychologically
3. Reinterpreting policy acceptance as a governance legitimacy problem
4. Demonstrating the structural limitation of post-regulatory governance

The paper moves beyond:

- generic privacy calculus approaches
- simple trust models
- descriptive policy acceptance studies

---

## Governance Contribution

The project contributes to governance research by showing that:

formal safety management mechanisms alone may not sufficiently generate legitimacy and trust in conditions of uncertainty.

This finding has implications for:

- disaster governance
- platform governance
- AI governance
- data governance systems

---

## Empirical Contribution

The study empirically demonstrates:

- trust has stronger explanatory power than safety perception alone
- institutional legitimacy matters significantly in policy acceptance
- post-regulatory systems may experience trust deficits

---

## Policy Contribution

The manuscript suggests that:
effective governance requires not only technical safety management,
but also institutional legitimacy and trust formation mechanisms.

The study therefore provides implications regarding:

- transparency
- accountability
- governance legitimacy
- institutional trust design

---

## Important Contribution Protection Rules

Do NOT weaken the contribution into:

- "trust matters"
- "people dislike risk"
- "privacy concerns affect acceptance"

The contribution must remain centered on:
the structural limitation of post-regulatory governance under uncertainty.
