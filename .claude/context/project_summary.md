# Project Summary

## Project Title

The Structural Limitation of Post-Regulation in Risk Society:
Disaster Personal Data Utilization and Platform Governance

---

## Core Research Problem

This project investigates whether post-regulatory governance systems can successfully generate public trust and policy acceptance regarding personal data utilization in disaster situations.

The study focuses on the structural limitation of post-regulation under conditions of uncertainty and risk society.

---

## Core Argument

The project argues that:

post-regulatory governance may structurally fail to generate public trust even when institutional safety management systems are formally implemented.

In disaster contexts, uncertainty, technological risk, and institutional distrust may weaken the effectiveness of post-regulatory approaches.

As a result, public acceptance of personal data utilization is shaped more strongly by trust than by perceived safety management alone.

---

## Research Context

This project integrates:

- risk society theory
- privacy regulation
- disaster governance
- platform governance
- institutional trust
- policy acceptance research

The study is NOT merely:

- a privacy acceptance survey
- a generic trust study
- a descriptive governance analysis

The study aims to explain:
why governance legitimacy becomes difficult to secure under post-regulatory systems in conditions of uncertainty.

---

## Key Variables

### Dependent Variable

- consent:
  willingness to provide personal information

### Independent Variables

- manage_trust:
  trust in institutional management systems

- risk:
  perceived risk regarding personal information utilization

### Mediator

- trust:
  institutional trust

### Additional Variable

- safety_perception:
  perception of safety management effectiveness

- Survey-based quantitative study
- N = 1,094

- Regression analysis
- Mediation analysis
- Moderation analysis
- Robustness checks
