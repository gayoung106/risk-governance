I am pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript entitled "Public Acceptance of Personal Data Use for Disaster Response: Associations with Management Trust and Risk Perception" for exclusive consideration for publication in the Journal of Risk Research. The manuscript examines how management trust and risk perception are associated with public acceptance of governmental personal-data use for disaster response within the broader context of disaster data governance.

I believe the manuscript is well suited to the Journal of Risk Research because it addresses a risk-governance issue that has become increasingly relevant as governments rely on personal information to manage infectious diseases, natural hazards, and other disasters. The use of personal data in disaster response can support rapid and coordinated public action, but it also raises concerns about privacy, leakage, and misuse. The manuscript therefore treats personal-data use not only as an administrative or technological matter, but also as an issue of public acceptance shaped by risk perception and trust. This focus aligns with the journal's interest in how risks are perceived, governed, and managed under conditions of uncertainty.

The central research question is how policy acceptance of personal-data use for disaster response is associated with management trust, understood as judgments regarding the necessity and effectiveness of data use, and how this association varies by the level of risk perception. Within the same analytical framework, the manuscript also considers positive perception of safe management and institutional trust. In doing so, it integrates privacy-calculus and trust-based perspectives while maintaining a cautious interpretation of the evidence.

Empirically, the study draws on a cross-sectional national survey of 1,094 Korean citizens. The analysis uses OLS regression together with mediation and moderation analyses to examine the associations among management trust, risk perception, positive perception of safe management, institutional trust, and policy acceptance. The principal findings indicate that management trust and positive perception of safe management are positively associated with policy acceptance, whereas risk perception is negatively associated with acceptance in the primary specification. The analysis also indicates that the positive association between management trust and policy acceptance becomes stronger as perceived risk increases, and that the indirect association through institutional trust is stronger at higher levels of perceived risk. These findings are interpreted as associations rather than causal effects, in keeping with the study's cross-sectional design.

The manuscript contributes to the risk governance literature by clarifying how public acceptance of disaster-related personal-data use may be associated with both perceived safety and citizens' judgments about the necessity and effectiveness of data use. Rather than treating privacy risk and trust as separate explanations, the analysis considers how they operate together within disaster data governance. The findings suggest that agencies may benefit from clearly communicating the purpose and effectiveness of personal-data use, particularly under conditions of higher perceived risk. This practical implication is presented conservatively and does not imply a causal prescription.

I confirm that the manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. As the sole author, I approve its submission to the Journal of Risk Research. I also confirm that there are no conflicts of interest. This research received no external funding.
