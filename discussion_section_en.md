# Discussion

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Restatement of Core Findings

This study analyzed how citizens' policy acceptance is associated with management trust, risk perception, and positive perception of safe management in the context of disaster-related personal information governance. Because the original coding of q25 was `1=yes` and `2=no`, this revised manuscript recoded the item as `safe_management=1` (perceives information as managed safely) and `0` (does not).

The revised interpretation is straightforward. Citizens who perceive their personal information as being managed safely tend to show higher acceptance of policies that use personal information for disaster response. This finding is consistent with general expectations in the prior literature, and the direction of the coefficient after recoding `safe_management` indicates a positive association with positive perception of safe management.

The central empirical structure of the manuscript is nevertheless maintained. First, management trust shows a comparatively strong positive unstandardized association with policy acceptance. Second, risk perception is negatively associated with policy acceptance. Third, institutional trust shows an exploratory indirect association pattern between management trust and policy acceptance. Fourth, under higher risk perception, the positive slope between management trust and policy acceptance is stronger.

### 5.2 Theoretical implications: observed associations and possible interpretations

Positive perception of safe management, based on the recoded q25 item, and management trust, based on q5 and q6 as perceptions of the necessity and effectiveness of data use, were both positively associated with policy acceptance. The unstandardized coefficient for management trust was comparatively strong, although the two coefficients should not be directly compared as evidence of relative magnitude because one variable is continuous and the other is binary.

This result is consistent with the interpretation that, when citizens evaluate policy, they may consider not only a status evaluation of whether information is currently being managed safely but also a purpose- and performance-based judgment about whether the use of information is necessary and effective. Beck (1992)'s theory of risk society explains the dual condition under which the use of personal information is both an essential resource for disaster response and a source of misuse risk. The tendency for risk perception to be negatively associated with policy acceptance, with the specification sensitivity noted in §4.4, is consistent with this account. In light of Luhmann (1979)'s theory of trust, management trust may facilitate judgment under conditions of uncertainty. However, the present data cannot determine whether citizens' judgments also include accountability, procedural fairness, or relational trust. These issues remain tasks for future research using multi-item scales.

### 5.3 Implications for the formation of policy acceptance

This study did not measure perceptions of governance legitimacy itself. The dependent variable is the level of agreement with the collection, disclosure, and sharing of personal information for disaster response, or policy acceptance. The preceding results, in which positive perception of safe management and management trust showed independent positive associations with policy acceptance, suggest that policy acceptance may not be reducible to a single evaluation of safety status.

### 5.4 Conditional association by risk level

The interaction between management trust and risk perception was statistically significant, and its direction and significance were retained across several alternative specifications. However, the incremental explanatory power was small (ΔR²=.0041, Cohen's f²=.0084), indicating that the moderating effect of risk level is statistically robust but substantively modest. The positive slope between management trust and policy acceptance was stronger under higher risk perception, and the conditional indirect association through institutional trust also increased as risk level rose.

By information type, the positive slopes between management trust and policy acceptance were significant for all 15 information types, but their magnitudes differed significantly across types. Similar differences by information or policy domain have been reported in prior research (Trein & Varone, 2024), but the present data cannot determine whether these differences reflect the objective sensitivity of the information. This remains a task for future research.

One possible interpretation is that, as risk perception increases, citizens may rely more heavily on management trust, that is, perceptions of the necessity and effectiveness of data use, rather than judging individual risks on their own. This moderating effect, however, does not identify the psychological process through which trust operates, such as uncertainty reduction or judgments about responsibility. Further research using experimental designs or multi-item scales is needed to examine these mechanisms.

The policy implication that may be drawn from this result is that, in contexts of high risk perception, explanations by disaster response agencies regarding the necessity and effectiveness of personal information use may be associated with policy acceptance. The cross-sectional design of this study, however, cannot determine whether such explanations actually change citizens' trust or acceptance. Specific institutional design elements, including accountable explanation, procedural fairness, and ex post remedies, were not measured in this study and should be examined separately in future research.

### 5.5 Distinction from existing research on privacy acceptance

Existing research on privacy calculus has explained personal information disclosure as an individual risk-benefit calculation. The present findings do not contradict this approach: risk perception is negatively associated with policy acceptance, and positive perception of safe management is positively associated with it, which is consistent with risk-benefit logic. At the same time, the preceding results show that management trust is conceptually distinct from positive perception of safe management and is independently, and comparatively strongly, associated with policy acceptance, although the coefficients should not be directly compared because the scales differ. This suggests an extension beyond a simple evaluation of safety status, in which policy acceptance may also be associated with judgments about the purpose of data use.

### 5.6 Policy implications

The implications below are tentative and based on observed associations; they are not presented as causal prescriptions.

The positive association between positive perception of safe management and policy acceptance suggests that the substantive quality and visibility of measures supporting citizens' perception that personal information is being managed safely may be related to policy acceptance. The independent positive association of management trust suggests that agencies' explanations of the necessity and effectiveness of personal information use may also be related to policy acceptance. Whether such explanations actually change acceptance, or whether accountable communication and procedural fairness additionally shape this relationship, was not measured in this study and should remain a hypothesis for future experimental research or studies using multi-item scales.

The finding that the management trust-policy acceptance slope is stronger under higher risk perception suggests, even given the small incremental explanatory power, that explanations of the purpose of data use may be relatively more relevant when perceived risk is high. This result alone does not provide sufficient grounds to prescribe the design of procedural fairness or ex post remedy systems. The effects of such institutional elements require separate testing.

### 5.7 Limitations and future research

First, because this study is based on cross-sectional data, it cannot establish causal relationships. Whether management trust increases policy acceptance, or whether respondents with higher policy acceptance place greater trust in agencies, should be examined further using longitudinal data or experimental designs.

Second, positive perception of safe management was measured using a single binary q25 item. This item provides a direct assessment of whether respondents believe that personal information is being managed safely, but it cannot distinguish among detailed dimensions such as transparency of the safety management system, technical safeguards, accountability, or the availability of ex post remedies. Future research should separate subdimensions of safe management perceptions using multi-item scales.

Third, institutional trust was also measured using a single q26 item. Subdimensions of trust in government agencies, including ability, integrity, procedural fairness, and value congruence, were not measured in this study.

Fourth, concepts discussed in this Discussion, including accountability, procedural fairness, relational trust in institutions, and institutional capability, were not directly measured by any survey item. These concepts are presented only as possible interpretive extensions suggested by the items measuring management trust (q5 and q6, perceptions of necessity and effectiveness) and institutional trust (q26, general trust). They should not be treated as empirical findings of this study. Testing these concepts requires the development of separate multi-item scales.

Fifth, this study is situated in the Korean context of disaster data governance. Korea's strong administrative capacity, experience with infectious disease response, and personal information protection environment may affect the interpretation of the findings. Future research should examine the generalizability of the results through cross-national comparisons or extensions to platform and AI governance domains.

### 5.8 Conclusion

The revised conclusion of this study is as follows. In disaster data governance, positive perception of safe management and management trust are both positively associated with policy acceptance. Management trust, however, showed a relatively stronger positive association with policy acceptance. Risk perception is negatively associated with policy acceptance, but under risk conditions, the positive slope between management trust and policy acceptance was observed to become stronger.

The study makes three contributions. First, it shows that, in disaster data governance, positive perception of safe management, understood as an evaluation of the current status, and management trust, understood as perceptions of the necessity and effectiveness of data use, are conceptually distinct and independently positively associated with policy acceptance. Second, it identifies a conditional pattern in which these associations vary by the level of risk perception, including both moderation and conditional indirect association. Third, it provides evidence of heterogeneity showing that these associations also differ significantly by information type. All three contributions are based on observed associations, and the underlying psychological and institutional mechanisms, such as accountability, procedural fairness, and relational trust, remain tasks for future research.
